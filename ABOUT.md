# About this analysis

**Who.** John G. Keating, Professor and Associate Dean for Teaching and Learning, Faculty of Science and Engineering,
Maynooth University ([ORCID 0000-0002-5063-2773](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5063-2773)).

**Status.** This is individual research and analysis. The questions, interpretations and any errors are my own. It is not an
official Maynooth University publication, it was not commissioned by the University, and it does not represent the
University's position or policy.

**Funding.** This work received no funding.

**Why.** The daily journey to college shapes who can study where, what it costs families, and what it adds to
transport emissions, yet it is rarely measured campus by campus across a whole region. Every input here is public, so
the same questions can be asked of any campus, and the answers checked by anyone.

**An interest to declare.** I work at Maynooth University, one of the fourteen campuses in the study, and Maynooth is
the worked example. Every campus is modelled with the same data and the same methods, every tool works for any of them,
and the report states plainly where Maynooth comes out poorly: far fewer young people can reach it within an hour by
public transport (42%) than by car (69%), and it recruits fewer students than its commute predicts
from some of the counties on its doorstep.

**How AI was used.** The study was built with Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding assistant, working under my direction.
It wrote most of the data-processing and modelling code and drafted much of the report's text. I set the questions,
made the analytical choices and reviewed the results. Every figure in the report is computed by the published scripts
from the published data; none is supplied by the AI. Two kinds of check guard against errors: every number in the
report was checked against the output tables before release (that check found and corrected a fault in the enrolment
model, recorded in the version history), and the travel-time model is compared with the journeys students report in
Census 2022. The code, data and checks are all released so that anyone can repeat them.

**Corrections.** If you find an error, please open an issue on the repository or email john.keating@mu.ie. Corrections
are made in a new version, listed in the version history, and each version keeps its own DOI, so earlier citations
remain valid.
